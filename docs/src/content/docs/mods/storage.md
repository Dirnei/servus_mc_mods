---
title: "Servus: Storage"
description: An in-world warehouse with crates, racks, rails, shuttles, a storage terminal, zones and work orders.
---

Servus: Storage (`servus_storage`) lets you build a real warehouse. There are no storage disks: items sit in crates and racks you can see, rails link them into one network, and shuttles drive along the rails to fetch and put away. It depends only on Servus: Foundation.

Numbers below are the current defaults. They live in datapack JSON under `data/<namespace>/storage/` (`crate.json`, `rack.json`, `warehouse.json`, `crafting_station.json`) and modpacks can change them.

## Storage blocks

- **Crate, Double Crate, Quad Crate**: bulk storage with one, two or four compartments that split a capacity of 4096 items. Each compartment holds one item type and shows it with its count on the front. Right click to insert, double right click to insert all, left click to take a stack.
- **Iron and wood**: the controller, terminals, stations, Access Point, Shuttle Garage, Crafting Station, Crafting CPU and Pattern Storage have a dark riveted iron frame with wooden panels, like the racks. Every one of their recipes takes planks, and the block shows the wood of those planks (the kind used most; oak when it has none). The block keeps its wood when mined and placed again, and the item tooltip names it.
- **Heavy Duty Rack**: a chest sized bay (27 slots) for mixed items with a GUI. Racks in a row join into sections of up to three bays and show the items they hold the most of on their shelves. The sides climb like a ladder. Racks are only served by shuttles.

## Warehouse blocks

- **Warehouse Controller**: exactly one per network makes it online. Indexes every storage block and every foreign inventory that touches one, polling a few sources per tick.
- **Storage Terminal**: sits on a data line, not on the rails. Search the stock of every warehouse it reaches, request an amount, push items in. Deliveries arrive as packets in its nine output slots. Also edits stock rules. Both terminals open with their content right away: a terminal you opened before in this session shows its last content at once, and the fresh content replaces it a moment later.
- **Warehouse Rail**: a flat floor rail that connects to neighboring rails and docks onto storage blocks and stations. Rails link everything they touch into one network and carry shuttles.
- **Rail Elevator**: stacked shaft segments that link rails on different floors. They work like scaffolding: stand on any level, sneak to sink down, jump inside to climb, and click any segment with an elevator to add one on top of the shaft.
- **Shuttle Garage**: holds up to four Shuttles. Its back rail is their home. More garages mean more shuttles.
- **Shuttle**: an item placed in a garage. Shuttles are simulated as data and drawn moving on the client.
- **Inbound Station**: an intake buffer. The warehouse puts its contents away, by shuttle when it has shuttles.
- **Access Point**: the warehouse's door to the data line. It sits beside a rail like a station and touches a duct with the data line. Packets that arrive are put away like an Inbound Station delivery; shipments to a block on the data line are picked into it (instantly from crates, by shuttle from racks) and leave as packets, one stream at a time. With several Access Points, a shipment uses the one with the shortest shuttle trip from the racks holding the item, so more Access Points mean more throughput.
- **Outbound Station**: keeps a configured set of items stocked in its buffer and pushes them into the block behind it, for pipe lines that run all the time. It takes no part in work orders.
- **Crafting CPU**: 27 CPU blocks in a 3x3x3 cube, formed with the Configuration Carrot. A formed CPU on the data line owns the work orders of that line and runs two at a time (`order_slots` in `storage/cpu.json`); more CPUs run more orders at once. Right click it to see its slots. Breaking a block unforms the cube and cancels its orders. Formed, every side of the cube reads as one panel with an iron frame around the edge and teal lights that run while the CPU has orders and glow slowly while it is idle.
- **Production Terminal**: the production overview on the data line, with three tabs. **Crafting** is a crafting monitor: one row per job with the item and amount, a progress bar and what it is doing in plain words (for example "Crafting Station: crafting 1 of 2", "Waiting for a CPU" or "Waiting: Furnace (adapter) out of fuel"); the red X cancels a job, and finished jobs fade out after a few seconds. The header counts the CPUs and their slots; hover it for each CPU. **Machines** is a status list: one row per producer or upkeep member with its icon, name, state (idle, crafting, out of fuel, blocked, no machine, no recipes, upkeep only) and an upkeep bar; problems come first, and the tooltip says how many items it can make. **Patterns** lists every pattern of the line with a capacity bar; the plus button opens the recipe editor (with your inventory below it and JEI transfer) and saves the pattern into the first Pattern Storage with room, and clicking a pattern opens it to change or delete it. Patterns are not items; you need nothing in your inventory. The lamp is lit while a CPU or a warehouse is on the line. Made from glass panes, copper ingots, a comparator, planks and redstone.
- **Pattern Storage**: keeps nine patterns (`patterns_per_block` in `storage/pattern_storage.json`) as data, each one vanilla crafting recipe with concrete items. It sits on the data line; more blocks hold more patterns. An empty hand shows how many it holds. Nine lights on its front show how many patterns it holds, and a lamp above them is lit while a CPU or a warehouse is on its line. Mined or dismantled, it keeps its patterns in the dropped item and restores them when placed again. Made from iron ingots, paper, redstone, a chest and planks.
- **Crafting Station**: a powerless crafter and the hands of the Crafting CPU. It has nothing to configure: a CPU hands it a pattern and a number of crafts, packets bring the inputs, and it crafts until the job is done. Its GUI shows the job's result and how many crafts are left, or idle. A lamp on its front is lit while a CPU or a warehouse is on its line, and its crafting grid lights up in turn while it crafts. Without a job it crafts nothing and hoppers and ducts cannot put items in. A station whose output slots still hold items that are not its current job's takes no new job: the CPU uses another station, and the Machines tab shows it as blocked (outputs not collected) until the outputs are empty. More stations craft more orders at the same time.
- **Zone Label**: an item with a name and an item or tag filter. Stamp it onto crates and racks; put-away fills matching zoned blocks first and uses unzoned blocks as overflow.

Every storage block that is part of a working warehouse shows a connection lamp.

The Storage Terminal, the Production Terminal, the Crafting Station, the Pattern Storage and every block of a formed Crafting CPU carry the data line themselves: blocks that touch each other share one data network, like devices in Refined Storage, and one duct anywhere links the whole group to the rest of the line. The Access Point, the Machine Adapter and the Warehouse Controller do not carry it, and a chest or machine touching only these devices does not join.

## Automation

- **Requests** at the Storage Terminal become pick jobs to the Access Point with the shortest shuttle trip: instant from crates and foreign inventories no shuttle reaches, by shuttle otherwise, never instant from a rack. The Access Point sends them on as packets. A terminal reaches a warehouse when the warehouse's controller or one of its Access Points shares its data line; with several warehouses the stock adds up and the fullest one ships first.
- **Work orders**: when a request cannot be filled and a producer on the data line (a Crafting Station, an Assembler) can make the item, a Crafting CPU of the line plans the production, reserves stock on hand, ships the inputs to the producer as packets through an Access Point and brings the outputs back the same way, where they are put away. Each producer runs one step at a time. A new order goes to the first CPU with a free slot. Without a CPU the shortfall stays missing. Requests, orders, station jobs and shipments are saved with their blocks and go on after a restart; a CPU whose chunk unloads pauses its orders and continues when it loads again. A pattern's result is what its recipe really crafts from the pattern's items, so blocks that take the wood of their planks (racks, the iron and wood blocks) come out with that wood. A CPU also plans with the patterns of its data line: any Crafting Station on the line can run any pattern, and a new order prefers a station that has no work planned yet.
- **Chains go machine to machine**: when one step of an order makes an ingredient for a later step (stone for stone bricks, planks for sticks), the CPU sends it as packets straight from the first machine to the next one instead of through storage. Only extras go to storage. A step that would wait for a busy machine moves to another free machine with the same recipe, so two Crafting Stations can share a chain. When the next machine is busy with another order, the ingredient waits there for a few seconds (`handoff_wait_ticks` in `storage/cpu.json`, 100 by default) and then goes to storage as before. The Production Terminal shows it as, for example, "Furnace (adapter): sending Stone to Crafting Station".
- **Crafting from the terminal**: the Storage Terminal's grid also lists everything a producer on its data line can make, from patterns and machine recipes. Items not in stock show "Craft"; craftable items in stock carry a small orange marker. Click a "Craft" item, or Ctrl click or middle click a craftable one in stock, to open the craft dialog: enter an amount and the terminal shows what the order would take from stock, what it crafts and what is missing. Start sends it to a Crafting CPU, and the results land in the terminal's output slots. Crafted items for a request travel straight from the machine to the terminal; only extra items from a batch and the parts made along the way are put away. Start stays off while materials are missing or no CPU is on the line. A right click on the search box clears it. When several recipes make the same item, such as charcoal from any log, the order uses the ingredients you have, and splits between them when one kind is not enough (for example 6 oak and 4 birch logs for 10 charcoal).
- **Terminal view**: the Storage Terminal looks and works like a Refined Storage grid. A column of buttons left of it sets the sort (name, amount, mod), the order (ascending, descending), the view (all, stock, craftable), the search (normal, or synced with JEI one way or both ways when JEI is installed) and the height (small, medium, tall, or auto to fill the screen; the grid never grows past the screen). These settings are yours: every Storage Terminal uses them and they stay across restarts. Open requests are the first entries of the grid, on a blue background with the delivered and requested amounts and a coloured mark for their state (white picking, teal sending, yellow waiting for room, green producing, red missing); hover one for the details and click it to cancel it. In the amount prompt and the craft dialog, the stack buttons go to full stacks first (+64 from 1 gives 64, -64 from 70 gives 64), and the -1, -10 and -stack buttons lower the amount, never below 1. The crafting grid sits left of the output slots.
- **Crafting grid**: the Storage Terminal also has a 3x3 crafting grid that works like a crafting table, with the warehouse as its pantry. After every craft each ingredient that was used is refilled from crates and other instant storage (not from Heavy Duty Racks, which only shuttles reach), so shift clicking the result crafts until the stock runs out. Alt click an item in the stock list to take a stack of it onto the cursor for the grid. The x button moves the grid back into the warehouse, and closing the terminal does the same. With JEI, the transfer button on a crafting recipe fills the grid, from the warehouse first and from your inventory second.
- **Stock rules**: a minimum and maximum per item. When stock drops below the minimum, a CPU on the warehouse's data line gets a work order that refills it up to the maximum. Without a CPU the rule reads "no CPU".
- **Commands**: `/servus_warehouse` has subcommands to inspect a network (`info`, `zones`, `rules`; `producers` and `orders` look at the data line) and to act on it (`take`, `feed`, `order`, `rule`, `unrule`, `ship <controller> <target> <item> <count>` to send items through an Access Point to a block on the data line).

## How it connects

- [Servus: Foundation](/servus_mc_mods/mods/foundation/): network graph, side configuration, the Configuration Carrot, the production and stock capabilities, and ducts that move items in and out of any storage block that exposes an item handler.
- [Manufacturing](/servus_mc_mods/mods/manufacturing/): not required. When installed, an Assembler on the data line is a producer, so Crafting CPUs plan work orders with its recipes, through the Foundation production capability.
