---
title: "Servus: Storage"
description: An in-world warehouse with racks, rails, shuttles, a storage terminal, zones and work orders.
---

Servus: Storage (`servus_storage`) lets you build a real warehouse. There are no storage disks: items sit in racks you can see, rails link them into one network, and shuttles drive along the rails to fetch and put away. Your first warehouse is a Heavy Duty Rack, a Warehouse Rail, a Shuttle Garage with a Mk1 shuttle and a Warehouse Controller; every pick and every put-away rides a shuttle. It depends only on Servus: Foundation.

Numbers below are the current defaults. They live in datapack JSON under `data/<namespace>/storage/` (`rack.json`, `warehouse.json`, `crafting_station.json`, `packing_station.json`) and modpacks can change them.

## Storage blocks

- **Iron and wood**: the controller, terminals, stations, Packing Station, Shuttle Garage, Shuttle Workshop, Crafting Station, Crafting CPU and Pattern Storage have a dark riveted iron frame with wooden panels, like the racks. Every one of their recipes takes planks, and the block shows the wood of those planks (the kind used most; oak when it has none). The block keeps its wood when mined and placed again, and the item tooltip names it.
- **Heavy Duty Rack**: a chest sized bay (27 slots) for mixed items with a GUI. Racks in a row join into sections of up to three bays and show the items they hold the most of on their shelves. The sides climb like a ladder. Racks are only served by shuttles, so a rack section joins the warehouse only through a rail in front of one of its bays; touching the controller or another section does not connect it. A rack is a big chest for hoppers and item pipes only while no rail stands in front of any bay of its section; lay a rail there and the section is served by shuttles only (hoppers stop, pipes lose their arm), break it and the rack opens again. You can always open a rack by hand.

## Warehouse blocks

- **Warehouse Controller**: exactly one per network makes it online. Indexes the racks of its warehouse, polling a few per tick; chests and other inventories that touch a warehouse block are never part of it. Every controller is a **site** (your base or an outpost). Right click it with an empty hand to open its GUI: type a name for the site (an unnamed one is called "Warehouse at x, z"), see whether it is online (hover the line for the shuttle summary), how many chunks the site spans and a small map of them, the controller's chunk in gold. The site counts every chunk that holds a block of the warehouse; a part that unloads still counts, and the count only shrinks while the whole site is loaded. The name and the stock rules stay in the controller when you dismantle it with the Configuration Carrot. Machines on the data line see the controller's stock counts, but nothing can be taken out of it or put into it directly: items leave and enter only by shuttle. Operators can still take items straight out of the racks with `/servus_warehouse take`.
- **Anchor Upgrade** (a Foundation item, also used by the Ender Anchor): goes into the controller's four upgrade slots (`anchor_upgrade_slots` in `warehouse.json`). Each one covers 9 chunks of the site (`chunks_per_upgrade`) and lets a sleeping site wake for transfers. When the site spans more chunks than its upgrades cover, the GUI says so in red, the map turns red and the controller's second lamp glows red; such a site never wakes. Without an upgrade there is nothing to exceed and the lamp stays green. Crafted from an Ender Anchor with an ender pearl above, iron ingots at the sides and redstone below. Upgrades drop when the controller is broken or dismantled.
- **Sleeping and waking sites**: the world remembers every site while nobody is near it: its name, team, chunks, upgrades and the stock it last reported (refreshed every 5 seconds while the whole site is loaded and online). A site with enough Anchor Upgrades can **wake** for a transfer: every chunk of the site loads, stays loaded while the work runs (shuttles out, jobs waiting, inbound buffers, held deliveries) and 30 more seconds (`wake_grace_seconds` in `warehouse.json`), then goes back to sleep. While it is awake the controller's second lamp glows orange and its GUI says so. A site without an upgrade or larger than its upgrades cover never wakes. While awake, every chunk of the site counts in the chunk load of the team that placed the controller, so it shares the Ender Anchor limit (49 chunks by default), each chunk once even when an anchor of the team holds it too; a site whose chunks would push the team over the limit does not wake, and with FTB Chunks installed it stays asleep like the Ender Anchor does; a wake that may not happen waits and the controller GUI says why. Wakes end with a restart. `/servus_warehouse wake <pos>` wakes the site whose controller is at the position, `/servus_warehouse sites` lists every site with its chunks, whether it is awake and how many item types it last reported.
- **Wireless Upgrade**: makes the controller the site's antenna. It goes into the controller's own Wireless Upgrade slot, apart from the Anchor Upgrades; one is all it takes, a second adds nothing. A controller is **wireless capable** while it holds the upgrade and the FE for one item at the smallest cost (4 FE, `wireless_fe_per_item` in `power.json`). Only then does the site trade with other sites of your team in the same dimension: it neither sends nor receives otherwise, other sites show its name in orange, and its controller GUI lists no other site but says what is missing ("Insert a Wireless Upgrade to trade with other sites" or the FE it needs). Crafted from an ender eye in the middle, a copper ingot above, iron ingots at the sides and redstone and an iron ingot below. Drops when the controller is broken or dismantled.
- **Controller FE**: the controller holds 100,000 FE (`controller_energy_capacity` in `power.json`), fed from any side through the Utility Duct power line or any other energy source; it never gives FE out. It is the site's only power connection: the whole warehouse runs on it (see Warehouse power below). The GUI shows the FE stored and the FE used during the last second, so you can size your generators. A dismantled controller loses its FE. Each item of a wireless transfer costs 4 FE plus 0.02 FE per block between the two controllers (`wireless_fe_per_item`, `wireless_fe_per_item_per_block`), paid by the **receiving** controller when the item moves; the sending site pays nothing for the wireless hop. Without enough FE at the receiver nothing moves and the transfer waits.
- **Packing Stations carry the wireless line**: every Packing Station on a site's rails sends and receives wireless transfers, also one without any duct (its GUI then says "No data line: serves wireless transfers only" as plain information). The sending station takes the picked items from its To network row and hands them to a Packing Station of the receiving site that reaches the requesting Storage Terminal by data line, the one with the fewest open incoming transfers. They land in its To network row (only while it has room) and go on as packets into that terminal's output slots, like a delivery of a request at this site; they are not put away. A transfer moves at the slower of the two stations' rates (8 items per second, more with Speed Upgrades), shared with their data line deliveries. A site without a Packing Station can neither send nor receive. A transfer whose sending or receiving controller stops being wireless capable stops, shows waiting for power, and goes on once both are capable again.
- **Other sites in the terminal**: while this site's controller is wireless capable, click the title of a Storage Terminal to cycle through the other sites of your team whose controller holds a Wireless Upgrade (right click goes back). Without the upgrade or power the title shows only this site, and a terminal that showed another site goes back to this one. An online site shows its live stock, a sleeping one its last known stock greyed out. A site whose controller has no power for wireless says so in red. Requesting an item there makes a transfer: the other site picks it by shuttle into one of its Packing Stations, sends it over the wireless line to a Packing Station here on the terminal's data line, and that station passes it on into the terminal's output slots like a request from this site's storage; nothing is put away here. While the output slots are full the items wait in that Packing Station and move on once you empty them. A sleeping site with enough Chunk Loader Upgrades wakes for the transfer and sleeps again after it; one without cannot be ordered from. The transfer shows with the requests (waking, picking, sending, waiting for room, waiting for power), leaves the list once the items are in the output slots, and a click cancels it. A wireless capable controller lists the other sites with their state at its side; hovering one shows its name and state.
- **Storage Terminal**: sits on a data line, not on the rails. Search the stock of every warehouse it reaches, request an amount, hand items in. Below the grid sit two rows: the **Input** row (its arrow points up, into the warehouse) sends whatever you put into it to the nearest Packing Station on the data line, which puts it away like any intake, racks included. The row empties only as fast as the Packing Station takes the items (8 per second) and only while its To storage buffer has room, so what it cannot take yet stays visible in the row; without a Packing Station on the line the items wait there. Shift clicking your inventory, clicking the grid with a carried stack and clearing the crafting grid all go through the Input row. The **Output** row (its arrow points down, to you) is where deliveries and crafted requests land. Hoppers and item pipes cannot reach the terminal (it has no item capability); data line packets arrive through its packet inlet: items an open request waits for go to the output, everything else through the input into the warehouse. While the terminal shows another site the Input row is closed, since what you hand in is stored at this site. The terminal's rows are never stock, also when it touches the controller. Also edits stock rules. Both terminals open with their content right away: a terminal you opened before in this session shows its last content at once, and the fresh content replaces it a moment later.
- **Warehouse Rail**: a low track that connects to neighboring rails and docks onto storage blocks and stations. Rails link everything they touch into one network and carry shuttles. Two steel rail heads run along it, far enough apart that the shuttles' wheels roll on them, over four wooden sleepers per block that stick out past the heads. The sleepers are the wood of the planks in the recipe (iron ingots in two columns with a plank between them, sixteen rails), the rail keeps that wood when you break or dismantle it, and rails of different woods do not stack. Curves bend both heads in a stepped arc, three or four rails meet in a crossing on a sleeper plate, and a dock ends the heads at a striped buffer beam.
- **Rail Elevator**: stacked shaft segments that link rails on different floors. They work like scaffolding: stand on any level, sneak to sink down, jump inside to climb, and click any segment with an elevator to add one on top of the shaft.
- **Elevator upgrades**: right click any segment of a shaft with an empty hand to open its upgrade slots (4, `lift_upgrade_slots` in `warehouse.json`). Only Speed Upgrades fit; each one adds 0.05 blocks per tick to the car's top speed (`lift_speed_per_upgrade`) and 0.0025 to its acceleration (`lift_accel_per_upgrade`). The lowest segment keeps them: a segment placed under the shaft takes them over, and when the lowest segment breaks the shaft above falls like scaffolding and the upgrades drop where it was.
- **Shuttle Garage**: holds up to four shuttles, with their upgrades and tools. The slot of a shuttle that is out on a job is locked until it is back. Its back rail is their home and its only link to the warehouse; touching the controller does not connect it. More garages mean more shuttles.
- **Shuttle Mk1, Mk2 and Mk3**: items placed in a garage. Shuttles are simulated as data and drawn moving on the client. The tier sets how many stacks a shuttle carries per trip (2, 4 and 8, in `storage/shuttle.json`): it takes the queued jobs that share its way, the stacks of one request from the same rack or the stacks of a station going to the same storage block. Mk1 is crafted; Mk2 and Mk3 are built in the Shuttle Workshop. A shuttle drives 0.15 blocks per tick and stands still at every stop where it loads or unloads: 10 ticks plus 4 per stack. The tooltip lists its tool and upgrades.
- **Shuttle looks**: a shuttle shows what it is, on the rails and as an item. Mk1 is an open wooden crate that shows its cargo, Mk2 a closed iron hold and Mk3 a closed diamond hold with gold corners; closed holds hide their cargo. The drive follows the Speed upgrades: four wheels, six wheels from 1, tracks from 3 and tracks with a rear spoiler from 6: a steel wing over the back of the hold with dark iron end plates, on two struts leaning back from the hold. Wheels and tracks stand on the two rail heads, and every wheel shows a dark disc with an iron hub below the body. Handling adds a loading mast with forks at the back from 1 and a crane over the hold from 4. A Harvester carries a reel and a cutter bar across its front, wider than the shuttle; a Spreader carries a hopper box on two arms at its back, behind the loading mast, with a bone white spreader bar across the back, also wider than the shuttle. A shuttle carries one tool.
- **Speed and Handling upgrades**: a shuttle takes the Foundation Speed Upgrade (each adds 0.04 blocks per tick to its top speed and a quarter more acceleration; a shuttle speeds up from standing and brakes before every stop, so fast shuttles need a longer run) and the Handling Upgrade (each makes stops and field work 20% shorter), as many of each as its tier allows (2, 4 and 8). The lift car keeps its own speed.
- **Shuttle Workshop**: the work bench for shuttles, a dark iron frame over boards in the wood of its planks with a wrench and a screwdriver hanging on every side. It keeps the shuttle you put in and the supplies beside it, and drops them when broken. Sneaking with the Configuration Carrot dismantles it into one item that keeps its wood, the shuttle and the supplies (the tooltip lists them); placing it brings them back, and a build by hand in progress is cancelled. Upgrades and tools go into the install slot: a stack installs as many as the shuttle takes, a new tool replaces the old one, which comes back. The GUI lists the tier, the upgrades with their max and the tool, each with a button that takes one out. It also shows what the next tier needs (Mk2: 16 gold ingots, 2 chests, 16 redstone; Mk3: 8 diamonds, 2 gold blocks, an ender chest); once the supplies are in, **Build now** turns the shuttle into the next tier with its upgrades, tool and name. **Build by hand** does the same build as a short game on a large view of the shuttle: tighten the glowing bolts in order, wire each contact to the contact of its colour, weld along the seam (the steps per tier are in `shuttle.json`, about twenty seconds in all). Nothing can go wrong, the shuttle and supplies stay locked until the build is done or cancelled, and closing the GUI keeps the progress. At the end you name the shuttle: the name plate is shown in its item name and in the Field Head while it works the field, and kept through later builds. Only builds make plates, so a Mk1 never has one.
- **Harvester**: a tool module that makes its shuttle a field worker; it never takes warehouse jobs. Every `field_check_interval` ticks (40, in `storage/field.json`) the warehouse looks at the heads of its fields and sends one harvester per field when a harvest is due, or when the field has a crop set, has water and has dirt to till or empty farmland to plant (with seeds in the head's buffer). The harvester drives out over the rails and stops at each farm rail with work: from there it works the plots nearest to that rail, out to the field radius, one plot per `work_ticks` ticks (10, shorter with Handling). It harvests ripe crops into its cargo, tills dirt and grass under empty plots and plants the field's crop; in harvest only mode it only harvests. Seeds come from the harvest first; only when nothing is ripe does it take the missing seeds, up to a stack, from the head's buffer at the farm rail beside the head, never from racks, and without seeds there it plants only what the harvest gave. Spare seeds fill the head's buffer up to its level before the rest is put away. It carries the stacks of its tier; when full it puts the harvest away like an Inbound Station would, zones included, and comes back while work remains. If nothing it can drive to has room it waits at its garage with the cargo. A Harvester is too wide for the lift car: it drives straight through a shaft on its own floor but never rides up or down, so its field, its garage and the storage it fills must share a floor. Storage it reaches only by lift is skipped, and a field it cannot reach without a lift shows "No harvester gets here without a lift" in its Field Head. Breaking the head ends the run (the harvest is still stored); breaking its rail or recalling the garage drops the cargo at the garage.
- **Spreader**: a second tool module for field work, crafted from iron ingots, a hopper, a dispenser and a bone block, and fitted in the Shuttle Workshop like the Harvester (a shuttle has one tool, so a shuttle is either a harvester or a spreader). It never takes warehouse jobs or harvest runs and, like the Harvester, it is too wide for the lift car. The Spreaders of a warehouse serve all its fields: any free one goes to any field that calls. A field calls when its Field Head has fertilizing on (Fertilizer tab), plots that need a dose and the fertilizer in its buffer, at the warehouse's field check, at most every `fertilize_interval` ticks (600, in `storage/field.json`). The Spreader first takes fertilizer from the head's buffer at the farm rail beside the head, never from racks: the plots that need a dose times `doses_per_plot` (2), but never more than the buffer or its cargo holds. It then drives rail by rail and gives each plot up to its doses, one fertilizer item per dose applied like bone meal; any item of the tag `servus_storage:fertilizer` (bone meal by default) counts as one bone meal. A plot gets no more doses once it is ripe, ripe plots get none at all, and the head sees the growth like bone meal used by hand. What is left goes back into the head's buffer up to its level, the rest into storage like a harvest, then the Spreader parks. Breaking the head or turning fertilizing off ends the run (the rest is still stored); breaking its rail or recalling the garage drops the cargo at the garage.
- **Supply runs**: every Field Head keeps a small buffer: seeds of its crop up to `seed_buffer` (64, in `storage/field.json`), its fertilizer up to `fertilizer_buffer` (64) while fertilizing is on, and anti-corrosion items up to `anti_corrosion_level` (8). Harvesters and Spreaders take only from that buffer, so stock in a rack they could only reach by lift still reaches the field. At every field check, when a part holds less than half its level and the warehouse holds that item (the fertilizer only above the head's Keep in stock reserve), the warehouse sends a shuttle without a tool on a supply run. Like a pick it may ride the lift car. It picks what each part is short of, low parts first and as far as its cargo holds, drives to the farm rail beside the head and fills the buffer. Items in the buffer that are no longer the field's crop or fertilizer go back with it and are put away. One supply run per head is out at a time. A warehouse whose garages hold only Harvesters and Spreaders sends none, and the head says no shuttle can bring supplies, with a red marker. Breaking the head during a run sends the shuttle to put its load away; breaking its rail, its garage or recalling the garage drops the load at the garage.
- **Produce and stock levels**: fields can produce on demand. Every Field Head learns its field's **produce** from its harvests: after a run that harvested, the produce is the item that run brought home the most of, leaving out the crop item whenever something else came too (wheat for a wheat field, not its seeds; carrots for a carrot field; in harvest only mode simply the most). Choosing another crop forgets the produce until the next harvest. Each head has two levels, counted in the produce in the warehouse of its field and read at every field check: the **harvest limit** ("Stop at" on the Field tab) and the **fertilize level** ("Below" on the Fertilizer tab). Both start at `harvest_limit` and `fertilize_below` (0, in `storage/field.json`), and 0 turns a level off, so a field harvests and fertilizes as before. While the warehouse holds at least the harvest limit, the field harvests nothing: no harvester leaves for ripe crops, and a harvester that goes out to till or plant leaves ripe plots standing, so the field is the store. One run may bring the stock above the limit; once orders take it below, the next field check sends the harvester again. While the warehouse holds at least the fertilize level, or while harvesting is paused at the limit, no Spreader is called and a Spreader already out stops dosing; below it the mode, threshold and reserve apply as before. The fertilize level is never above the harvest limit while both are set: moving one past the other moves the other along. Until the head has a produce, both levels do nothing. Each level row has -64, -1, +1 and +64 buttons, shift click moves ten times as far, and the row's tooltip names the produce and its stock. The field command prints the produce, its stock and both levels.
- **Farm Rail**: a Warehouse Rail for fields with copper rail heads (fresh copper with a touch of verdigris) on wooden sleepers, crafted like the Warehouse Rail with copper ingots instead of iron. It is a rail in every way: it links into the warehouse and shuttles drive over it. One connected run of farm rails is one field; Warehouse Rails do not extend a field. Its copper ages with the field in five stages: copper, exposed, weathered, oxidized and corroded, the whole field at once. A crafted rail is always fresh copper; placed into a field it takes the field's stage (when two fields join, the one with the Field Head decides, without a head the younger stage wins), and a rail you break and place again on its own is fresh. Right click any rail of a field with an axe to scrape the whole field back one stage, with the vanilla scrape sound: it costs one axe durability per rail, and an axe with less durability left than the field has rails does nothing and tells you what it needs. Fresh copper cannot be scraped. Warehouse Rails are steel and never corrode.
- **Field Head**: one per field, placed beside a farm rail. It takes water through the fluid pipe (or any fluid handler) into a buffer of `capacity` mB (8000, in `storage/field.json`) and uses `water_per_rail` mB (1) per rail every `water_interval` ticks (100). While it has water, farmland one block below the rails stays wet within the field radius of each rail. The radius is `radius` (2, in `storage/field.json`) to every side; each Range Upgrade in the head's upgrade slot adds one, up to `max_radius` (4), and the field's plots, water and ripe share follow at once. Breaking the head drops the upgrades. It counts the crops on the field and the ripe ones from growth, bone meal and breaking, never by scanning, and shows Harvest due once `harvest_share` (half) of the planted crops are ripe and the field has a crop or harvest only set. Its GUI has four tabs on the left edge, with your inventory always below: **Field** has the crop picker: a button with the field's crop that opens a list of every item that plants a crop, mods' crops included, with a search field and "None, harvest only" first. Each entry shows how many the warehouse holds; an entry with none stays selectable but is greyed, because the first planting needs seeds in the warehouse. You do not need the seed in your inventory to choose it. The Field tab also shows the seeds in the head's buffer right of the picker (count and level), the ripe count with its bar, the harvest threshold, the harvest limit with the produce icon, the produce with its stock ("Produce learned at the first harvest" before the first harvest) and what the field is doing, including farmland that waits for a supply run with seeds or that no shuttle without a tool can supply, and "Harvest paused: 1,024 / 1,000 Wheat" while the stock is at the limit; **Water** has the water bar, hydrated or dry, the anti-corrosion items in the buffer with their level and the copper stage; **Fertilizer** has a fertilizer picker like the crop picker ("Off" first, then every item of the fertilizer tag with its warehouse stock), the mode buttons **Off**, **After planting** (every crop planted since its last dose gets one round of doses; crops already standing when you switch it on do not count) and **Until ripe** (unripe crops get doses while the ripe share is below the head's own threshold, `fertilize_share` half at the start, set with - and +), the **Keep in stock** reserve with -10, -1, +1 and +10 (`fertilizer_reserve` 64 at the start; supply runs never take the stock at or below it, and a field with 0 may use it all), the fertilizer in the buffer right of the picker, the fertilize level and a status line: fertilizing off, Spreader at work (with its name plate), waiting, no Spreader shuttle, no Spreader that gets there without a lift, no fertilizer in stock, stock at or below the reserve, no shuttle that can bring supplies, the buffer empty while a supply run is on its way, or not needed while the produce stock is at the fertilize level or harvesting is paused; **Upgrades** has the Range Upgrade slot, the radius and the field size. Shift click puts Range Upgrades into their slot from any tab; the buffer takes nothing from you and gives nothing back. A tab turns red while it holds a problem (no field or a work warning on Field, a dry or corroded field on Water, and on Fertilizer while fertilizing is on and there is no Spreader, none that gets there, or no shuttle that can bring supplies; no fertilizer in stock, stock at the reserve, a paused harvest or fertilizing that is not needed is shown without red). A second head on the same field stands aside. It also counts the empty plots on dirt or grass (to till) and on farmland (to plant). The head is built like a station: board panels in the wood of the planks it was crafted from under an iron frame, and its front faces you when you place it. Its block shows what it knows: a glass window in the upper panel of every side shows the water buffer filling up, the top shows the field's crop on a dark plate (empty without a crop or in harvest only mode), and a small bar on the front fills with wheat gold as the ripe share approaches the head's harvest threshold and turns green once a harvest is due. The head also drives the copper of its field: every `corrosion_water` mB (64000, in `storage/field.json`) of water it uses for hydration one aging step is due. If the anti-corrosion part of its buffer holds an item of the tag `servus_storage:anti_corrosion` (honeycomb by default), one is used and the stage stays, so you can keep a stage you like; otherwise the field turns one stage older. Supply runs keep that part at `anti_corrosion_level` (8) of the item it holds, or of the first item of the tag while it is empty. You cannot fill it by hand, and hoppers, item pipes and the data line cannot reach it: the head is not on the data line. The GUI shows the stage and how far the next step is. A corroded field is no longer watered: the farmland may dry, the head uses no water and so ages no further, its GUI says "Rails corroded, no watering" and its top plate gets a rust red rim. Harvest runs go on; scrape the rails with an axe to water the field again. Breaking the head drops its buffer.
- **Inbound Station**: an intake buffer. Shuttles put its contents away; without a shuttle the stack waits and the station says it needs a shuttle. Item pipes connect to it: a duct shows an arm toward every side that is not Off and feeds it straight in, and a Pull side empties the inventory touching it. Like every block shuttles serve (racks, stations, Packing Stations, garages) it joins the warehouse only through a rail touching it, never by touching the controller or another station.
- **Packing Station**: packs items from the warehouse into packets for the data line, and unpacks arriving packets for storage. It sits beside a rail like a station and touches a duct with the data line. It sets the throughput between the warehouse and the data line: it converts `items_per_second` (8, in `storage/packing_station.json`) in each direction, and the two directions do not slow each other down. It has `upgrade_slots` (2) for Speed Upgrades, the only slots of its GUI you can change: each Speed Upgrade doubles its rate, so two make 32 per second. The data cable on its path can limit it too (see the bandwidth of the [cable tiers](/servus_mc_mods/mods/foundation/)). Arriving items land in its **To storage** buffer and are put away like an Inbound Station delivery; a full buffer takes nothing more, so the items wait at their source. Shipments to a block on the data line are picked into its **To network** buffer by shuttle from the racks and leave at its rate, one delivery at a time. Packets reach it through its packet inlet; hoppers and item pipes cannot reach it. Right click shows both buffers read-only, the upgrade row with the current rate, a tooltip for each row and a status line that names the current delivery or why the station waits (no rail, no data line, waiting for a shuttle, To storage full); nothing can be taken out, and breaking the block drops both buffers and its upgrades. With several Packing Stations, a shipment uses the one with the shortest shuttle trip from the racks holding the item, and each station adds its own rate.
- **Outbound Station**: keeps a configured set of items stocked in its buffer (restocked by shuttle) and pushes them into the block behind it, for pipe lines that run all the time. Item pipes connect to its Push and Output sides: a duct beside a Push side shows an arm and the station pushes into it. It takes no part in work orders.
- **Crafting CPU**: a box you build from Crafting CPU blocks and CPU Buffer blocks and form with the Configuration Carrot. Any solid box without gaps works, every side at least 2 blocks long (the smallest is 2x2x2), with at least one CPU block. Each CPU block adds an order slot (`orders_per_cpu_block` in `storage/cpu.json`) and each Buffer block adds one stack of buffer room (`stacks_per_buffer_block`); a box can have up to 512 blocks (`max_blocks`). When the carrot cannot form it, the action bar says why: a gap (with its position), not a box (an L shape or a missing corner), too small, no CPU block, or too big. Only unformed blocks join, so a new box built against a formed CPU forms on its own, and two unformed boxes that together fill a box form one CPU. CPU and Buffer blocks place against each other without sneaking. A formed CPU on the data line owns the work orders of that line; more CPUs run more orders at once. Right click it to see its slots and buffer stacks in use. Breaking a block unforms the box and cancels its orders; whatever waits in its buffer goes as packets to a Packing Station of the line and is put away by shuttle (what the station cannot take drops beside it, and without a Packing Station it drops at the broken block). Formed, every side of the box reads as one panel with an iron frame around the edge, teal lights on CPU blocks and amber lights on Buffer blocks, which run while the CPU has orders and glow slowly while it is idle. The CPU has no item capability: item pipes and hoppers attach to nothing and cannot put items into it; only packets of its own orders get in. Made from iron ingots, redstone, planks and a crafting table (CPU blocks) or a chest (Buffer blocks), four per craft.
- **Production Terminal**: the production overview on the data line, with four tabs. **Crafting** is a crafting monitor: one row per job with the item and amount, a progress bar and what it is doing in plain words (for example "Crafting Station: crafting 1 of 2", "Waiting for a CPU" or "Waiting: Furnace (adapter) out of fuel"); the red X cancels a job, and finished jobs fade out after a few seconds. Under a job, a line names what waits in its CPU's buffer for a later step ("In CPU buffer for Crafting Station: 4 Oak Planks"), and a red line says when a full buffer sent intermediates to storage ("Buffer full: 16 Oak Planks went to storage"). The header counts the CPUs and their slots; hover it for each CPU. **CPUs** lists every formed CPU of the line with its position and size, a bar of order slots in use with the number of waiting jobs, a bar of buffer stacks in use and the items in the buffer (cut short with a count of the rest); the tooltip lists every buffered item with its job, and items of finished or cancelled jobs that wait to leave for storage are marked. A CPU without Buffer blocks says it has no buffer. **Machines** is a status list: one row per producer or upkeep member with its icon, name, state (idle, crafting, out of fuel, blocked, no machine, no recipes, upkeep only) and an upkeep bar; problems come first, and the tooltip says how many items it can make. Below them a **Data line** section names the busiest duct of the line with its load ("Busiest duct: Data Cable, 30 of 32 items/s") and lists every Packing Station with how much of its rate it used during the last second to network and to storage; a direction at its full rate is marked busy. **Patterns** lists every pattern of the line with a capacity bar; the plus button opens the recipe editor (with your inventory below it and JEI transfer) and saves the pattern into the first Pattern Storage with room, and clicking a pattern opens it to change or delete it. Patterns are not items; you need nothing in your inventory. The lamp is lit while a CPU or a warehouse is on the line. Made from glass panes, copper ingots, a comparator, planks and redstone.
- **Pattern Storage**: keeps nine patterns (`patterns_per_block` in `storage/pattern_storage.json`) as data, each one vanilla crafting recipe with concrete items. It sits on the data line; more blocks hold more patterns. An empty hand shows how many it holds. Nine lights on its front show how many patterns it holds, and a lamp above them is lit while a CPU or a warehouse is on its line. Mined or dismantled, it keeps its patterns in the dropped item and restores them when placed again. Made from iron ingots, paper, redstone, a chest and planks.
- **Crafting Station**: the hands of the Crafting CPU, paid with FE from its warehouse's controller while it works. It has nothing to configure: a CPU hands it a pattern and a number of crafts, packets bring the inputs, and it crafts until the job is done. Its GUI shows the job's result and how many crafts are left, or idle. A lamp on its front is lit while a CPU or a warehouse is on its line, and its crafting grid lights up in turn while it crafts. Without a job it crafts nothing and takes no packets. It has no item capability, so hoppers and item pipes cannot reach it; packets come in through its packet inlet. Its sides are Push (the default: once no job holds them, its outputs go into the inventory on that side), Pull (it takes items its job uses from the inventory on that side) or Off. A station whose output slots still hold items that are not its current job's takes no new job: the CPU uses another station, and the Machines tab shows it as blocked (outputs not collected) until the outputs are empty. More stations craft more orders at the same time.
- **Zone Label**: an item with a name and an item or tag filter. Stamp it onto racks; put-away fills matching zoned racks first (racks already holding the item first) and uses unzoned racks as overflow. The controller takes no zone.

Every storage block that is part of a working warehouse shows a connection lamp.

The Storage Terminal, the Production Terminal, the Crafting Station, the Pattern Storage and every block of a formed Crafting CPU carry the data line themselves: blocks that touch each other share one data network, like devices in Refined Storage, and one duct anywhere links the whole group to the rest of the line. The Packing Station, the Machine Adapter and the Warehouse Controller do not carry it, and a chest or machine touching only these devices does not join.

## Warehouse power

The warehouse runs on FE through its controller, one power connection per site. Nothing costs FE while idle: storing items, racks, rails, garages, stations, terminals and a CPU without running steps draw nothing. What does cost FE, paid from the controller's buffer (numbers in `storage/power.json`):

- **Shuttles**: 20 FE for every block of rail or farm rail a shuttle starts to drive (`shuttle_fe_per_block`), whatever the job, the way home included. A lift ride costs the same per block of height, paid in full when the car takes the shuttle. Standing, loading, unloading and field work at a plot are free.
- **Crafting Stations**: 5 FE for every tick a station works on a craft (`crafting_station_fe_per_tick`), paid by the controller of the first warehouse its data line reaches. Waiting for inputs or for room is free. Steps on Machine Adapters and Manufacturing machines run on their own fuel and power and draw nothing from the controller; the CPU itself draws nothing.
- **Waking**: 2 FE per chunk of the site for every tick a wake holds it loaded (`wake_fe_per_chunk_per_tick`), awake or in its grace time. A site loaded by players, Ender Anchors or anything else that loads chunks pays nothing for being loaded.
- **Wireless**: per item and distance, paid by the receiving controller (see above).

A draw is paid in full or not at all. While the buffer is empty or a draw was refused during the last second the controller is in the **no power** state: its lamp glows red, its GUI says the warehouse has no power and its automation waits, and every Storage Terminal on the site shows a red no power line. The state is a warning and stops nothing by itself; each consumer stops only when its own draw is refused:

- a shuttle stops at the start of the block it cannot pay, with its cargo and its jobs, and drives on once the block is paid; a shuttle that cannot pay its ride waits beside the shaft; while the state lasts no shuttle leaves its garage for a new job or field run;
- a Crafting Station pauses with its progress kept, its GUI says "Waiting for power" and the Production Terminal shows the step waiting for power; the order keeps its reservations and buffered items;
- a sleeping site wakes only when its controller held at least one second of its wake cost when it went to sleep (the site record keeps that FE); a wake whose tick cannot be paid ends at once, releases its chunks and says no power. A refused shuttle draw never ends a wake.

Everything resumes by itself once FE comes back. The stock stays visible in the terminal, requests are still taken (they show "waiting for power" while their shuttles or stations wait), and racks still open by hand. For scale: a Coal Generator makes 40 FE per tick; a Mk1 shuttle driving spends about 3 FE per tick, a working Crafting Station 5, a woken 9 chunk site 18.

## Automation

- **Requests** at the Storage Terminal become shuttle pick jobs from the racks to the Packing Station with the shortest shuttle trip. Without a shuttle that reaches the racks a request is refused with "needs a shuttle". The Packing Station sends them on at its rate. A terminal reaches a warehouse when the warehouse's controller or one of its Packing Stations shares its data line; with several warehouses the stock adds up and the fullest one ships first.
- **Work orders**: when a request cannot be filled and a producer on the data line (a Crafting Station, an Assembler) can make the item, a Crafting CPU of the line plans the production, reserves stock on hand, ships the inputs to the producer as packets through a Packing Station and brings the outputs back the same way, where they are put away. Each producer runs one step at a time. A new order goes to the first CPU with a free slot. Without a CPU the shortfall stays missing. Requests, orders, station jobs and shipments are saved with their blocks and go on after a restart; a CPU whose chunk unloads pauses its orders and continues when it loads again. A pattern's result is what its recipe really crafts from the pattern's items, so blocks that take the wood of their planks (racks, the iron and wood blocks) come out with that wood. A CPU also plans with the patterns of its data line: any Crafting Station on the line can run any pattern, and a new order prefers a station that has no work planned yet.
- **Chains go machine to machine**: when one step of an order makes an ingredient for a later step (stone for stone bricks, planks for sticks), the CPU sends it as packets straight from the first machine to the next one instead of through storage. Only extras go to storage. A step that would wait for a busy machine moves to another free machine with the same recipe, so two Crafting Stations can share a chain. When the next step runs on the same machine, or its machine is busy with another order, the ingredient waits in the CPU's buffer and goes back to the machine as packets when the step runs, so a chain such as oak logs to planks to a crafting table on one Crafting Station never sends the planks to storage. Buffered items belong to their order: they are no stock for other orders and do not show in the Storage Terminal. Only when the buffer is full do they go to storage, after waiting at a busy machine for a few seconds (`handoff_wait_ticks` in `storage/cpu.json`, 100 by default); the order notes it and still completes. Items of a finished or cancelled order leave the buffer for storage. The Production Terminal shows it as, for example, "Furnace (adapter): sending Stone to Crafting Station".
- **Crafting from the terminal**: the Storage Terminal's grid also lists everything a producer on its data line can make, from patterns and machine recipes. Items not in stock show "Craft"; craftable items in stock carry a small orange marker. Click a "Craft" item, or Ctrl click or middle click a craftable one in stock, to open the craft dialog: enter an amount and the terminal shows what the order would take from stock, what it crafts and what is missing. Start sends it to a Crafting CPU, and the results land in the terminal's output slots. Crafted items for a request travel straight from the machine to the terminal; only extra items from a batch and the parts made along the way are put away. Start stays off while materials are missing or no CPU is on the line. A right click on the search box clears it. When several recipes make the same item, such as charcoal from any log, the order uses the ingredients you have, and splits between them when one kind is not enough (for example 6 oak and 4 birch logs for 10 charcoal).
- **Terminal view**: the Storage Terminal looks and works like a Refined Storage grid. A column of buttons left of it sets the sort (name, amount, mod), the order (ascending, descending), the view (all, stock, craftable), the search (normal, or synced with JEI one way or both ways when JEI is installed) and the height (small, medium, tall, or auto to fill the screen; the grid never grows past the screen). These settings are yours: every Storage Terminal uses them and they stay across restarts. Open requests are the first entries of the grid, on a blue background with the delivered and requested amounts and a coloured mark for their state (white picking, teal sending, yellow waiting for room, green producing, red missing); hover one for the details and click it to cancel it. In the amount prompt and the craft dialog, the stack buttons go to full stacks first (+64 from 1 gives 64, -64 from 70 gives 64), and the -1, -10 and -stack buttons lower the amount, never below 1.
- **Crafting grid**: the **Craft** button on the Storage Terminal's title row opens a popup over the item grid with a 3x3 crafting grid that works like a crafting table with what you put into it; pressing it again (or Escape) closes the popup and stores what is left in the grid. The grid is not refilled from the warehouse, whose racks only shuttles reach. The x button moves the grid back into the warehouse, and closing the terminal does the same. With JEI, the transfer button on a crafting recipe opens the popup and fills the grid from your inventory.
- **Stock rules**: a minimum and maximum per item. When stock drops below the minimum, a CPU on the warehouse's data line gets a work order that refills it up to the maximum. Without a CPU the rule reads "no CPU".
- **Commands**: `/servus_warehouse` has subcommands to inspect a network (`info`, which also names the site with its chunk count and capacity, `zones`, `rules`; `producers` and `orders` look at the data line) and to act on it (`take`, `feed`, `order`, `rule`, `unrule`, `ship <controller> <target> <item> <count>` to send items through a Packing Station to a block on the data line; `field <pos>` prints a Field Head with its counts, fertilize settings and status, its buffer and corrosion stage, `field <pos> recheck` reads its plots again and `field <pos> age <mB>` counts that much water toward aging, to try the stages quickly).

## How it connects

- [Servus: Foundation](/servus_mc_mods/mods/foundation/): network graph, side configuration, the Configuration Carrot, the production and stock capabilities, and ducts that move items in and out of any storage block that exposes an item handler.
- [Manufacturing](/servus_mc_mods/mods/manufacturing/): not required. When installed, an Assembler on the data line is a producer, so Crafting CPUs plan work orders with its recipes, through the Foundation production capability.
